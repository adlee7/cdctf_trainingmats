What is a CTF?

Capture The Flag (CTF) competitions are cybersecurity challenges where competitors solve puzzles to find hidden strings called "flags," usually formatted like cdctf{some_text_here}. Flags are submitted to a scoring platform to earn points.

Most CTFs follow a "jeopardy-style" format, where challenges are organized so teams can pick whichever challenges they want to attempt in whatever order they like — as opposed to "attack-defense" formats, where teams actively attack each other's infrastructure in real time. CDCTF follows this jeopardy-style approach.

Challenge Structure & Topic Tags

CDCTF doesn't sort challenges into the standard fixed categories you might see at other events. Instead, challenges are grouped under event-specific names, and each challenge is labeled with one or more topic tags that tell you which familiar skillset it draws on.

Think of a topic tag as a hint about what kind of thinking the challenge rewards, not a strict bucket. A single challenge can carry more than one tag if it blends skills.

Common Topic Tags

Web Crypto Pwn Reverse Engineering Forensics OSINT Log Analysis Scanning Network Analysis Social Engineering AI

How to Use Tags

  • Identify the skills needed for challenges in lieu of traditional categories.
  • Use tags to spot a weak spot in your team's coverage (e.g. nobody has touched anything tagged Crypto yet)
  • Don't assume a challenge's name tells you its tag — always check the tag(s) itself before diving in

Training MAT Challenges

Our Training MAT challenges are designed to give beginners a concrete place to start instead of facing a wall of advanced challenges with no entry point. Each MAT walks through the basic tools and reasoning for one skill area, so you leave with a working method you can apply to harder challenges in that same area later in the event.

If you're new to a topic, starting with its Training MAT is the recommended first move — this tutorial you're completing right now is itself part of a Training MAT.

How Dynamic Scoring Works

In response to feedback from last year's CDCTF, this year CDCTF will use dynamic scoring: every challenge starts at a maximum point value, and that value decreases as more teams solve it, down to a set minimum.

Why We Use It

  • Prevents "easy" challenges with hundreds of solves from being worth as much as genuinely hard ones
  • Encourages teams to spread out across categories instead of everyone rushing the same challenge
  • Organizers do not need to hand-tune every point value in advance

What This Means for You

A challenge's displayed point value can change throughout the event as more teams solve it, so don't be surprised if it's worth less by the time you finish than when you started. Check the scoreboard periodically to see which categories still have high-value, unsolved challenges.

At the beginning of the competition, all challenges will appear to be the same difficulty. If this seems daunting, start with the Training MATs and wait for the experienced competitors to solve challenges, revealing the easier ones in time.

Most challenges will have a starting value of 500 points and go down to 100, however a few challenges identified as extremely difficult may have a starting value of 800 points and go down to 400. If you solved a challenge at 500 points and it goes down to 300 with more solves, you will only retain 300 points. This means that during the competition your overall score may go down as more challenges get solves, but don't fret! Keep playing, and you'll keep gaining more points.

First, second, and third blood bonuses

This year our scoring system implements small first, second, and third blood bonuses.

  • First solve: +15 points
  • Second solve: +10 points
  • Third solve: +5 points

These are small bonuses mainly designed to break ties, reward technical skills, and discourage flag hoarding. All submission activity is monitored, any suspicious activity against our AI policy or rules may be subject to investigation. Your team may be asked to provide a writeup to prove that your solve(s) was(were) done by a human and not an AI/LLM in order to be eligible for full credit.

EXCEPTIONS: Some challenges are ineligible for blood bonuses - such as the Welcome challenge, Training challenges or challenges labeled as "bonus."

Unlike last year, bonuses are automatically applied in the CTFd platform.

Event AI Policy

CDCTF is made for humans, by humans. Our challenges are meant to be unique and creative (not especially challenging) with the goal of inspiring and fueling your love and passion for cyber. Except for challenges that explicitly state that they are made with AI (often to demonstrate the associated risks), all challenges are developed and solved manually by our team. If our verification team can solve these challenges themselves, you can too! And we encourage you to do so.

As for what is outright banned, our stance amounts to: you may use AI tools, but AI may not be the one solving the challenges. Since platitudes do not make an enforceable policy, here are our lines.

What's Allowed

  • Asking an AI tool to explain a concept, error message, or piece of documentation
  • Having an AI research the subject of a challenge
  • Designing a script to solve a challenge locally with AI assistance
  • Brainstorming challenge methodology with the help of AI
  • Using AI as a study aid before or between challenges

What's Not Allowed

  • Using AI tools to directly interact with our competition platform or challenge instances.
  • Pasting an entire challenge prompt or file into an AI tool and submitting its output as your flag without doing the analysis yourself
  • Using AI-powered automated exploitation tools that solve a challenge end-to-end with no human input
  • Sharing flags, challenge files, or solutions with AI tools or services in a way that leaks them to other competitors
  • Run an AI-generated script against our competition platform or challenge instances
  • Otherwise violate the competition rules under the guidance of AI

Enforcement

We will collect evidence throughout the competition.

  • If conduct appears suspicious, but may be benign, we will provide a warning indicating what we have observed.
    • If this warning was issued on arguable grounds (e.g. submission of a red-herring flag), a writeup may be provided as an appeal to have the warning disregarded.
    • This appeal must be submitted before disqualification.
    • If further misconduct proceeds before submission and the team is disqualified, that decision will not be reversed.
  • If warnings are not heeded and there is sufficient evidence of misconduct, we will disqualify the team.
  • If automated tools interfere with other teams or launch an attack on our infrastructure, we will disqualify the team without warning and block the associated IP addresses.

When in doubt, ask yourself: "Am I using this tool to learn and build my own solution, or am I using it to skip the challenge entirely?" If it's the latter, it's against the spirit of the event even if a specific rule doesn't name it. Organizers reserve the right to disqualify submissions that violate this policy.

Before You Continue

Ask Yourself

  • Would I be comfortable explaining my solution step-by-step to a teammate?
